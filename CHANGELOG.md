# Changelog
## 0.5.9 — unreleased

### AI commanders

- The skill and spotting speed set in Better Battle Buddies' Settings tab now reach AC: Actual's squads. AC: Actual sets its soldiers' skill and spotting every second and put its own over BBB's; now BBB's values stand in for the mode's (`-acrSkill`, `-acrReaction`), while a mission's pinned soldiers and a side's or profile's own skill still come first. Needs Better Battle Buddies' update.
- Vehicle manners, with Better Battle Buddies' new vehicle options (all off by default):
  - Get out off the road (`-acrPullOver 1`): a squad told to get out on a road first has its driver pull onto the verge on his right, then everyone gets out with the handbrake on. Under fire, or with nowhere to pull over, they get out where they stand as before.
  - Unarmed vehicles get away from armed enemies (`-acrEvadeArmour 1`, with BBB's `bbbEvadeArmour`): an unarmed vehicle of ours whose crew sees an armed enemy vehicle drives 300 m away from it, then carries on with its order once nothing armed has been seen for 15 s, or waits if the order leads past the enemy. Not while a player leads or rides with the squad.
- Fields of fire (host option `-acrFieldsOfFire 1`, off by default): a gun or a post that is built clears the bushes round it, so the men behind the sandbags can see out. All round a gun, a roadblock's post or a Fortify piece close in, and further out in a cone in front of a gun; trees are left to the existing tree felling. The cut branches vanilla's medium and large checkpoints carry as camouflage count too: two of them sit either side of the MG nest, just in front of it. Every machine clears the same bushes, so players in multiplayer see what the AI sees.
- Gunners on held guns (Fortify nests, checkpoint and roadblock guns, FOB and base defence guns) no longer sit on a gun with no ammo (#154): once it runs dry the gunner gets off and fights on foot nearby, nobody is put back on it, and it is manned again when it has ammo (with Better Battle Buddies' update). Held guns also carry six more boxes than the gun holds (an M2: 1,000 rounds instead of 400): the gun's spare boxes are topped back up as its gunner loads them, until that reserve is spent.
- Fortified towns differ. Each town now gets a defence style from its ground, what threatens it and its side's doctrine, weighted and seeded per side: a wall across each approach, strongpoints well apart with gaps on purpose, a thin forward line with the real one close in behind it, a funnel of lines across the ways in with the guns on them, or for a small place a ring close in all the way round. Host option `-acrBaseStyles 0` makes every town a wall.
- Fortified towns have more guns and less straight wall. Where a big MG nest has no room on a line, the game's small gun position takes the place of one piece, so cramped towns get a gun (host option `-acrBaseSmallGuns 0` turns those off). Most long lines also step in or out every 5 to 9 pieces with fire bays, so a man sees along them; which lines, how often and which way are drawn per line and seeded (`-acrBaseBays 0` turns those off).
- Fortify positions and checkpoints tie into the ground. A position or a checkpoint a squad builds now has sandbag wings running out from its ends: to the nearest wall, building or rock within 30 m (20 m for a checkpoint), or bent back to cover the flank, or on along the line and ending in a tail. Each wing is picked from what's there, weighted and seeded, so no two look alike. Checkpoints keep their lane clear, a gun going in at an upgrade takes the place of the bags where it stands, and the wings go when the work does. Host option `-acrWings 0` turns them off.
- Every panel on the command map moves: drag the ACA panel, INTEL, the Squads list and the 2IC panel by their title bars (lit under the cursor), and the 2IC's card and channel, the layer switches (the legend goes with them) and the debug box by their faces, or a folded panel by its bar. The place is kept per player and a double click puts it back. The Squads list follows INTEL until you drag it, and its fold is now a small Hide / Show button in its title bar. A game hint is never hidden behind a panel: while one shows it is drawn over them and stands beside any panel in its way, and the panels stay where you put them (before, a moved panel covered it, and so did one too tall to fit under it). If panels fill the top of the screen, radio lines stand below them. An armed order's words stand in a box beside the ACA panel, where a tall panel can't push them off the screen. Off the map, the F keys show as a reminder along the top of the screen, drawn as they are bound; **Key reminders** on the ACA panel, or a key under Settings > Controls > AC: Actual, hides it (a player's request). With a squad selected, the panel's build orders (FOB, roadblocks, mines, Fortify) fold under one **Build orders** button, so it stays short; the choice is kept, and they open by themselves while one is armed. Radio and chat lines stand beside the panels instead of under them.
- The ACA panel no longer runs off the bottom of the screen, whatever is selected and at either UI size (a playtester's report: a FOB's buttons were out of reach). A FOB's eight upgrades fold under one button that counts them, Report, 3D Camera, Key reminders and UI size fold under **More**, and each fold is remembered per player. An open list gives way first; if the rest still can't fit, the wheel scrolls everything under the panel's head, with a mark on its edge. A FOB's words no longer repeat what its buttons say, and a FOB no longer shows the last squad's "AI commands this squad" button.
- Fortify a whole base (#105). The Fortify cursor on the middle of a base we hold has a squad work the town stretch by stretch, one tier per order, paying as it goes: Defences I (sandbag lines across the approaches the enemy would use), II (MG nests built into the lines, more line, wire in front, a checkpoint in the lane where a line meets the road from the nearest enemy bases), III (bunkers and towers at the lines' ends or behind them, a line behind the first if the town has the depth, the command post). From Defences II a reserve deploys when an enemy comes near and goes behind the bags of the lines facing him, each man to his own place. A man whose way to his place comes to nothing (he stops dead on the walk there, often in a town on the way) is sent again, up to three times. A stretch with pieces shot away can be repaired: the next order puts back those pieces, at their share of the price. A line is one thing: sandbags end to end along the ground, from cover to cover where it can (a building, a wall, a gap in a fence, a slope) and curving back where it can't, stopping at a road's edge and going on across it with the lane left open, with a gap for men on foot every 30 m or so, and it goes on to the nearest wall, building or big rock before it gives up. Nests sit in a line with its bags reaching their wings, bunkers at its ends, towers right behind it, and a checkpoint stands in the lane with the line's bags meeting its sides. A squad that stops dead on its walk to a site is sent on: the same order again, then its leader stepped ahead. The worst approach gets its line first, and it looks at the town's buildings, walls, fences, roads and what already stands there to decide, differently from one town to the next. A gun needs a clear line of fire the way it faces, and nothing stands close to a door or on a road; a town with few good places gets a shorter line, not scattered bits. A new order refunds only the stretch in hand and the next one goes on with what's left, lost stretches first. The works stay when the base falls, for whoever took it. With `-acrFortifyAI 1` AI commanders do it at threatened front bases with RP banked, and a Quick start's biggest front town starts with Defences I. `-acrBaseWorks 0` turns it off.
- AI infantry ride on long trips and keep their orders on the way. A squad the AI commander posts somewhere is now committed to that trip: other plans (attacks, FOB escorts, reserves, new postings) leave it alone until it gets there, stops, meets the enemy, or the base it was going to is lost. A base under threat can still call it. Before, squads were pulled out of their trucks mid-way into another job and walked 5-8 km for half an hour. A squad that sets off on foot because no truck was free keeps asking for one on the way, so a truck freed or bought later picks it up. A squad re-tasked while riding is driven to the new place instead of being put out to walk. A truck fetched from far away gets time to arrive: trucks 1-1.5 km off used to give up after 4 minutes, and the squad walked. A ride's transport or carrier is no longer taken to build a FOB in the middle of a ride.
- Convoys: a squad too big for any one free vehicle rides in two or three together (jeeps, Humvees, UAZs). Players' Ride orders and the AI commander both use this. The first vehicle leads; the others meet it at the pickup and close up at the drop-off, and one that is lost or given other orders on the way just leaves the convoy.
- AI squads and Better Battle Buddies share the fight instead of taking turns (#101, host option `-acrSquadTactics 1`, still off by default). With it on, squads moving, attacking or hunting fight their way forward with BBB's squad tactics (assault, flank, fire and manoeuvre) inside their order's corridor or the objective's ring, defenders and waiting squads fight around their base, and riding, forced and transport moves are left alone. Before, most squads in contact were kept out of BBB's tactics, and those let in could often only hold. An attack no longer counts the time its squads spend gaining ground, or holding because they're outnumbered or flanked, as stalling (the hard time limit still counts everything), and a squad pinned outnumbered or flanked gets mortar fire on where its men last saw the enemy when the side has a mortar ready and no friendlies are near the spot.
- FIA guerrillas have room on a Quick start. Every base handed out counted as a starting base, which FIA keeps 1.5 km from, and with most of the map handed out that left almost nowhere: 4-8 cells of 14 and hardly a house. Now only the sides' own starting bases count (every base still keeps its own circle clear): 12-14 cells.
- A squad stops waiting for men who can't come. A soldier stuck somewhere stopped his squad for a minute every 3.5 minutes for the rest of the match, and the meeting point drew it back towards him. A man who stays out of a whole regroup without moving no longer counts, for stopping or for the meeting point, until he moves again.
- Bigger maps, bigger armies (host option, off by default): `-acrCapScale 1` multiplies every side's AI squad cap and the soldier cap by the map's bases / 12 or the spread of its bases / 5 km, whichever is more, between x1 and x2 (soldier cap at most 250). The active-AI limit follows, and the log line `[ACR] CAP SCALE` shows what it chose. The setup screen has a row for it: **Squad caps scale with map** (off / on).
- An AI mortar team that can't reach the base it was sent to isn't sent there again for 15 minutes. Before, a mortar team could be sent to the same unreachable base over and over, packing up and setting up again at home each time, and fired little all match.
- An AI vehicle whose driver got out on his own while the gunner stayed aboard gets its driver back: he climbs into the driver's seat (placed there if he hasn't made it in 30 seconds). Before, nothing put him back, and the commander sent the driverless vehicle on order after order that failed where it stood (a Typhoon in a playtest, for 37 minutes). A crew that left a vehicle with an empty tank waits for the fuel truck instead of getting back in.
- AI vehicles that keep failing to drive are left alone for a while: a supply truck that gave up stuck, or gave up a drive it couldn't find a way for, isn't sent on another run for 10 minutes, and a crew whose driver gets out three times in 10 minutes isn't put back in for 10 minutes, so it can walk its order. Before, a stuck arsenal truck was sent out 59 times in a 2.5-hour game, and a BTR crew got out and was put back in at every order for 90 minutes without the BTR moving.
- AI infantry keep off the roads outside friendly ground. Squads on foot walked the roads between bases, so two squads meeting on one lay on the same open strip and shot it out until one side was gone. A squad on foot crossing ground that isn't friendly now keeps at least 45 m off the roads (except near its destination), with a waypoint every 60 m: the game's own pathfinding rates a road three times cheaper than a field and cut back onto it between waypoints further apart (on a test road, a squad on one waypoint spent 77% of its walk on the road, one on the new route 16%). On its own side's ground it still uses them. A river on the way is crossed on the nearest bridge, walked onto and off along its road, and no waypoint puts a river between itself and the one before; before, squads kept off the roads waded or swam beside the bridge (West Zagoria). A river with no bridge near is left to the game's own path, as before. With Better Battle Buddies, a soldier caught on a road in a firefight also gets off it, but not on a bridge. Host option `-acrFieldRoutes 0` turns the route change off.

### Generated maps

- The scenario generator handles more community terrains, for the new ACA map add-ons (Chernarus Minus, Gulfcoast Islands, Kunar Province, West Zagoria, Zimnitrita). On island maps, where the widest-apart HQ pairs can sit on islands with no land route between them, it now takes closer pairs, and a half or flashpoint draw that can't make a playable layout tries other draws from the same seed. Before, such a map could start with no bases at all. Place names with stray tabs or line breaks are tidied, and placeholder names like "?" no longer become objectives. Layouts on Everon, Arland and Kolguyev are unchanged.

### Mod support

- Content packs (#108): an optional add-on can now bring vehicle and kit mods into the buy lists cleanly. Vehicle mods add every variant they ship to the game's vehicle lists (test vehicles, fictional upgrades, copies crewed by the other side), and ACA used to offer them all. A plugged-in pack decides which of those stay, adds its own vetted entries with a price and role, and names the crews: a bought vehicle from the pack is crewed by the buying side's own crewmen, with a driver even where the mod left the seat empty. A pack can also swap the soldiers a side spawns (squads, riders, garrisons and the insurgent cells drawn from them) and its command unit, so era kit reaches the whole army. Without a pack nothing changes. The first pack, the Cold War pack, is a separate add-on.
- Content packs bring attack helicopters to the support menu. A side whose pack has one (Cold War: AH-1S Cobra for the US, Mi-24V Hind for the Soviets; Modern: AH-64D Apache and Mi-24V Hind for the RHS sides) gets a new call-in named after it: the helicopter flies in with the side's own pilots, works the target for two minutes with its guns in attack passes and 24 rockets, and leaves (55 RP, 5 min cooldown). On maps with helipads it is bought for a pad like the gunship ("Support attack helicopter", 100 RP, 2 per match) and flies from there. The AI commander calls it for its assaults and to defend a base it is losing, and on helipad maps keeps an attack helicopter instead of a gunship when it can buy one. Without a pack the supports are unchanged.

### Fixes

- A squad waiting for a convoy of trucks no longer waits for good. The lead truck stopped by the squad and waited for the others; if the squad then moved off (still walking to the pickup, its leader killed, moving under fire), the trucks sat where they had stopped, out of use, while the squad waited for a ride that never came. Now the lead drives on to where the squad is and the pickup carries on (or runs out of time and the squad walks, as before).
- A squad holding a Fortify position stands behind its sandbags, not in front of them (#155). The game's Defend sent the men it had no place for round the edge of the hold, the front one well past the bags. Now every man not on a gun gets his own place behind a sandbag position's wall, standing at it to fire over or crouched behind, and the position's reserve, when it deploys, goes behind the bags too instead of round the position's middle.
- The arsenal at a built FOB's ammo dump works for players again (#156). Since FOB supplies moved into the command post's storage, nothing but FOB construction could draw on them, so the ammo dump's arsenal (and the FOB's other supply-using service points) had no supplies to give. Arsenals and service points inside the FOB now draw on the FOB's supplies, the same balance construction uses. Supply trucks still can't load from it.
- A squad told to garrison a building gets into it when one man is stuck on the way (#128). The game hands out the posts only once every soldier is near the building, so a man jammed at a fence or doorway kept the whole squad milling about outside with no posts (in the test, 3 of 9 on posts after a minute). A man who hasn't moved for 8 seconds short of the building no longer holds the others back.
- On the calm command map a unit marker is where its unit is, or it isn't on the map. Markers used to be moved to make room: squads at a base stood under its dot, and any icon on a base's name or dot was pushed aside, so zoomed out at a town three markers stood round its dot looking like real positions, and zoomed in they were somewhere else. Now, when a base's ring is too small on screen to tell who stands where in it, or a marker would touch the dot, the unit goes into a count beside the dot, outlined in the base's colour: "×4 units", and "· ×2 enemy" in red for contacts on it. A click on the count, or on the base, lists them in a dark box beside the cursor, clear of the panels and on the screen: name, kind, what each is doing, how far out when outside the ring, health and ammo, and the enemy seen there. Picking a row selects that squad, and a selected squad's icon always stands where it really is. The count also shows a fighting mark and the worst hurt or short-of-ammo dot of its squads. A base's name now gives way to icons instead of the other way round, and an order line starts where its squad really is. Squads standing on top of each other out in the open still show as one icon with "×N". `-acrMapDeclutter 0` keeps the classic map.
- The calm command map hides what it means to again (#14): the soldiers of a squad at a base (unless it is selected), our soldiers on a base's name, and the squads under a "×N" icon. The game shows every map marker again each frame, so a hidden one came back before it was drawn: a base with squads at it showed all their soldiers' dots around its marker, and the squads behind a "×N" sat stacked under its icon, where the cursor could catch one of them. Now each goes off the map until it is shown again.
- FIA ambushes no longer lie inside what stands on the ground. Their hide sites and men went by terrain height alone, so a bunker's mound or a gravel heap could swallow them.
- Squads and vehicles no longer spawn inside sand and gravel piles, rubble heaps, hangars and other buildings (#87). The game's search for open ground often hands out a spot inside a pile, and squads, bought units, reinforcements and spawn points took its spots without a second look (base garrisons already had one since 0.5.8). A vehicle could also get the empty floor of a hangar (a starting Humvee on a generated Kolguyev layout). Each of those now also looks down from above and skips a spot with anything but trees over it, and a crowded base looks further out for clear ground instead of keeping a spot nobody checked. Bought squads stand on their own clear ground, and a squad the game lays out in its formation is checked once its men are in: anyone inside something steps to clear ground nearby.
- Squads on foot get into walled objectives they can't reach from their side (#30). Some Everon base centres (Gallows Base, Chotain, Kermovan, Figari) sit in walled yards the game can path into from some sides only, and a squad coming from the wrong side gave up as unreachable. Now it goes round: back out the way it came, round to a flank, and in from there; the other flank if that fails too.
- The squad holding a fortified position stays behind it (#93). Its hold reached 10 m past the wire, and in a playtest a squad stood in the field in front of its own sandbags. It now holds a 16 m circle centred 8 m behind the line, whose front edge is at the sandbags, and Better Battle Buddies' own moves stay inside it. AI positions also keep off fences and buildings: a fence or wall within 25 m in front, or within 6 m on any side, and a building within 15 m on any side, now count against a spot (before, only a structure over 2.5 m high in front did, so farm fences and a building beside the spot never counted).
- AI vehicles on long road trips drive through instead of stopping and starting at every route dot (#86). The game's driver brakes and plans a new path each time its waypoint changes, and a road trip was split into a waypoint every 160 m. Now a crewed vehicle skips the dots on fairly straight stretches (up to 600 m ahead, keeping those at bends and junctions), gets each next dot as a queued waypoint so it flows on to it, and gets it earlier the faster it goes. In the test, a truck on a winding 2 km road stopped about once per km instead of 2-3 times, and no longer took a long wrong turn halfway that left it short of the end. `-acrRoadFlow 0` brings back the old driving.
- Roadblocks keep a lane for vehicles. A checkpoint's dragon's teeth, wire and signs left gaps too tight for a truck, so a side's own convoys jammed at its checkpoint, backed up, re-routed and gave up, and ran over the men holding it (pn12d playtest: the AI US side's medium checkpoint on its supply road). Now every solid piece in a 4.8 m chicane lane through the roadblock is slid aside across the road (a plain barricade's two halves are staggered 6 m apart into the same chicane), the gun stays beside the lane in its sandbags, the holders and defenders post off the road beside it (the builders wait on that side, so they cross the road before the wire is up), the side's own AI drivers take the lane at 12 km/h, and the men holding it step out of its way when one of the side's vehicles comes through. A vehicle still has to weave through it, 6-7.5 m from the gun. The checkpoint's camouflage bushes and stop sign go out to the verge instead of standing in the middle of the road, and the gun nest's loose sandbags, ammo and chair move with it.
- AI vehicles drive each trip on one waypoint, as the game's own scenarios do, and the game's driver plans the whole way there (#86). The road hops are only a fallback now: a vehicle whose drive fails (stuck, or no way found) out of contact keeps its crew aboard instead of the game putting them out to walk, and goes on over the road hops from where it stands. A trip ends within 22 m of its point instead of creeping for the last 5 m. The stuck watcher is calmer: it waits 35 s instead of 15, doesn't count up to a minute of queueing behind another of the side's stopped vehicles, never backs into a vehicle right behind, and doesn't re-issue a waypoint that is still there. Under fire, or with an empty tank, a crew that can't drive on still gets out as before. `-acrNoRoadHops 0` brings back the hops on every long trip.
- A squad holding a gun now leaves it when it gets a new order, every time. Better Battle Buddies mans an empty held gun again after 8 seconds; if it had sent a soldier to the gun a moment before the order, he still climbed back on, and the squad stayed put. A new order now calls off that soldier too.
- Structures on a slope now look the same to every player. The ground tilt laid a composition's pieces on the ground only on the server; a client built it level from its prefab, so in a multiplayer game a remote player could see sandbags and barricades where the server's weren't. The server now records the final layout on the composition and every machine lays the pieces out from it.
- A Get out order from the map panel gets men out of an LAV's rear seats too: those still aboard after 13 s go through the same steps as at a ride's drop (the action queue cleared, out without a door, another seat), and everyone is out within about 22 s. Before, only a ride's drop did that, and a player's Get out left two men of a squad aboard until the stuck-soldier fallback set them down beside it (playtest).
- A support helicopter or air lift crippled in the air really comes down. The script let go of it and gravity came back, but its engine kept running and the game's own flight model flew it on: crippled at 83 m it climbed to 232 m in 40 s, and one from a pad flew 1.8 km away before being removed in mid-air, while the side was told it was going down. The engine now stops.
- An AI crew that can't get back into its vehicle stops being told to, for 10 minutes after 3 tries. Each try was a new order every 90 s for as long as the crew stood near the vehicle: in a 2-hour match a Humvee's crew was told 14 times in 20 minutes, and a base grab was cut short on the way.
- Base defences see the far end of a long building. A building only counted within 40 m of its origin plus the distance asked for, and a big one's walls reach further (61 m on Kolguyev), so a position could be planned up against the wall of one.
- A gun crew from a position's reserve takes one gun each. A seat only shows as taken once a man sits in it, so all of them went for the first free gun and only later tries filled the rest: 4 guns took 21 to 26 s or more, now 6 to 11 s.
- The after-action report counts vehicles lost. The game clears a vehicle's faction when its last occupant leaves, so a vehicle destroyed after its crew died or bailed out was nobody's loss, and it wasn't on the side's timeline either: in two 2-hour matches, 8 vehicles destroyed and 0 lost.
- A support helicopter pushed from where it was set down goes back there before it lifts. It took off from wherever it stood, and in a 2-hour match a gunship 33 m off its pad held under a light tower for good, "out on a mission" with nothing flying, so the side couldn't use it or buy another.
- A player's Ride order can take the side's own APC or IFV (a LAV-25, a BTR-70: a fighting vehicle with room for a squad), crewed, gun manned, and not busy, as well as a transport. AI-ordered rides still never take a fighting vehicle, and an armed jeep isn't a carrier for either. A refused ride now names the nearest vehicle and why it can't come, e.g. "the nearest, LAV-25 ..., 15 m away: a fighting vehicle (manned gun)". Before, the Ride said "has a manned gun", which read as the LAV having no seats (playtest).
- A single-spot air lift (two helicopters taking turns) no longer sends chalk 1 back. Once landed, the men it carried walked back to the pickup, and chalk 2's boarding gave them seats there; now the squad holds when chalk 1 lifts off, and chalk 2 boards only those still at the pickup and leaves for home at once when nobody is left.
- An air lift with a man knocked out leaves once everyone awake is aboard. It waited for him for the full two minutes, though nobody puts an unconscious man aboard.
- A player who hands a squad to the AI keeps his trucks where he parked them. The AI sent every idle transport of the side back to a hub every 15 seconds, not only the squads handed to it.
- A paid mortar mission is refunded when the tube is destroyed or the team is wiped out before a round leaves.
- A squad holding a roadblock that was dismantled (a shovel, Game Master) stops holding an empty road. It stayed "Holding roadblock" all match, the AI counted it as a holder and never freed it, and it kept sending tripwire alerts.
- A vehicle that got past one stuck spot may re-route at the next one too; before, every later stuck spot on the same order went straight to "Stuck, holding".
- The AI commander keeps its FOB project when the builder squad stops to regroup on the way, instead of giving the site up, dropping its escort and avoiding the site for 10 minutes.
- With the 2IC on, a squad the player claimed back is out of the AI's attacks and defences: after taking command back, the AI's plans kept giving it orders that overrode his. A squad the 2IC hands to a player's request no longer ends its old attack with a false "Couldn't take" line.
- AI sappers walking up to an HQ are ordered there at most every 20 seconds (it was every second, which kept an unreachable HQ from ever being given up).
- F6 (drop the passengers) works for the driver or pilot and the commander only. Any rider in the back could throw the side's AI squads out of a flying helicopter.
- Squads with a construction truck can be taken over from the map again (F7). Their "can establish FOB" flag was the same as the commander-marker flag, so F7 skipped them. The commander marker has its own flag now, and no longer offers Establish FOB.
- The capture bar and on-screen ping markers clear when your soldier dies; they stayed frozen over the deploy screen until you spawned.
- The 3D camera's base card shows the base's own supplies (other bases showed numbers from the first base's stock line), and "none" without a FOB.
- When a possessed soldier dies and the takeover of the next man is refused, you go back to your command unit instead of staying in the body with a "Now controlling" message. Pressing F8 while already in the command unit no longer takes the map out of your hand.
- A restarted mission starts clean in more places (statics outlive a restart in the same server): the forced-side spawn point (`-acrPlayers`) is placed again, a starting company kit still waiting stops instead of firing into the next match, the debug Think card closes, `-acrMarksmen` and the setup screen's waiting hint are read again, and the FIA's last alliances are forgotten before the next setup.
- Hardening: a drag-aim or gunner-drive input that isn't a number is ignored, and Establish FOB site requests are rate-limited.
- Skirmish layouts (half, flashpoint) on a hand-built world no longer hand a side's starting units to the enemy: they took the side of the base nearest where they stood, which could be the enemy's new HQ. On a generated-only world the world's own starting units take the side of the nearest player spawn point (all of them went to the west HQ).
- The generator tries ordinary sites as HQs when no pair of military or airport sites works (two on different islands, or two labels on one airfield), instead of leaving the map with no bases. Maps that planned before keep their layouts.
- Two generated places with the same name are told apart ("Name 2"): moving or deleting one in the setup editor moved or deleted both.
- A generated layout that fails plays the world as placed, helipads included (every side got off-map helicopters).
- The match report no longer counts destroying an empty vehicle (a wreck, a civilian car, one's own abandoned truck) as an enemy vehicle destroyed.
- On/off host options accept off/on, false/true and no/yes as well as 0/1 (`-acrMines off` left mines on).
- A mission end, restart or rotation no longer logs an engine replication error for every soldier in an ACA squad.
- A support helicopter whose pad is lost (base captured, built pad dismantled) moves its base to another of the side's free pads. It sat on the old pad unusable for the rest of the match, the support list said "buy one", the AI never bought another, and the pad's new owner couldn't use the pad.
- Gunships, door gunners, rocket runs and ambushes leave FIA alone when FIA is allied to (or friendly with) their side.
- A FOB whose HQ was destroyed during an upgrade goes away once nothing of it is standing; before, the paused upgrade kept the empty FOB on the map and no new FOB could be built within 120 m.
- A supply delivery on its way when a FOB's HQ is destroyed is refunded (it was lost without a word), and buying at a FOB with no HQ says so instead of asking for a radio relay.
- AI and Quick Start minefields keep their 60 m from the side's own dug-in positions and roadblocks where the road is: the spot was checked before the field was moved onto the road through it, which could bring it a few metres inside.

### Players and spawning

- Choose your side: players now pick a side, an officer loadout and where to deploy on the game's deploy screen once the host presses Start, so both sides can be played by people (#39). Before, everyone spawned as a West commander unless the host forced the whole lobby onto one side. An AI commander still runs any side with no player in command, so player-vs-player, co-op and mixed games all work from the same scenario.
- Deploy at any base your side holds that can deploy units (HQs, military sites and built FOBs by default): each has a spawn point named after it on the deploy map, closed while the base is contested or the FOB is offline. The HQ is the default, and a side that has lost everywhere else can always fall back to its HQ point.
- Each player has their own respawn timer (the host's respawn delay), shown on the deploy screen. Before, one dead commander held up the whole side's spawn point.
- You stay on the side you first deployed on for the whole match, reconnects included. Hosts and admins can move a player with `/acr side west|east <player>`.
- The scenarios offer 16 player slots (8 per side). They advertised 8 before.
- In a solo game, the war now waits while the game is paused (the Esc menu, and the deploy screen in a solo game). Before, AI commanders kept buying squads that couldn't appear while paused, and those purchases timed out and were refunded.

### AI commanders

- AI commanders refill worn-down squads before buying new units: a squad back at a base gets its replacements even if that dips into the fire-support reserve, and new purchases wait while one is waiting for the RP. Before, refills had to leave the whole reserve untouched, so in a long Everon game one side refilled 3 squads and bought 21 units while squads of 1-3 men sat at bases.
- AI attacks on a held base need infantry that can walk in and take it: recon teams don't make up an attack, an attack whose infantry is killed or falls back is called off at once, one whose squads never got near their staging point is called off instead of sending a lone squad in from far out, and a base of fire goes in itself when it is the last squad left. Before, a counterattack could be a single recon vehicle 2.5 km away that overwatched 400 m short of the base until the attack timed out.
- AI squads on foot take a covered way to their staging point: when the direct line runs in view of the objective, they swing round it out of sight (behind a rise, through dead ground) as long as the detour stays reasonable, and the forming up waits for the longer walk. Staging points move a little further round or out where the objective can't see them. Before, a flanking squad walked the shortest path across the front of the objective.
- A soldier stranded off walkable ground (on top of a sandbag pile, say) steps back onto it when his move order keeps getting dropped, and an AI squad that can't reach the base it was sent to for reinforcements gives up after a minute instead of being re-sent every 15 seconds. Before, one RU machine gunner on a FOB's sandbags lost every order for over half an hour.
- The AI commander doesn't send a medic team or another support team to grab an empty base any more, as it already kept them out of attacks (#34).
- An AI attack or grab whose squads all report the target unreachable is called off at once, so they get new orders within seconds. Before, the commander waited out the attack's full time limit: on Everon a grab of Gallows Base, which can't be walked into from the south, kept its squad standing idle for 17 minutes.
- AI mortar teams follow the fighting: they move up to the own base nearest the enemy's known bases and the bases being attacked, instead of staying at the HQ whenever any unclaimed site happened to be in range (on Everon both teams sat at their HQs all game and fired one mission in two hours).
- A changed AI doctrine gets one review to settle in before it is judged, so doctrines change at most every 20 minutes. Before, a side taking losses could flip between Firepower and Fortress every 10 minutes.
- An AI doctrine that changed is kept at least 30 minutes, and the AI never switches straight back to the doctrine it just left for the same kind of reason (#29): losing ground and heavy losses count as one reason, and every counter as one. Before, a side that kept bleeding swapped between Fortress and Firepower at every review it was judged, and two AI sides could chase each other's counters all match.
- AI doctrine reviews don't count bases taken as winning while the side is losing men heavily: early on most bases taken are empty, and a commander losing 20 men for 4 kills used to think its doctrine was working.
- AI defences are built, upgraded and manned (playtest: one roadblock and one empty hasty position all game). Roadblocks, minefields and new Fortify positions go to whichever of the side's three bases nearest the enemy lacks one, not only the single nearest base. Its positions are upgraded (lowest tier first) before new ones are dug. One minefield is laid at a time, and no squad (AI or player) is sent to lay a field where another of its side is already on its way to lay one. With no free squad near the base (450 m for a roadblock, 600 m for mines, 500 m for Fortify), the nearest free squad within 1.5 km takes the job; past the doctrine's ride distance it rides out in a truck when one is free and is given, and pays for, the job at the drop. A squad on its way to refill isn't sent. A builder now stays on the roadblock or position it built (up to 2 per side); those squads aren't sent on attacks, and leave to defend a base only when their side is outnumbered there, or as a last resort. A lower cap keeps those holding longest. An empty position at a front base (its holder killed, or gone to defend) is manned again by the nearest free squad while the doctrine's holders allow, and only positions in use (manned, or at a front base) count towards the doctrine's number, so ones the front has left behind no longer stop new ones. A manned tier II position counts its holders half again towards its base's defence, a tier III twice, so the AI values keeping it manned. With the build budget on, engineering is paid out of it, and it gets at least 2 RP every 15 s on top of its share. A **Fortress** AI builds and mans more: twice the budget's share and trickle, up to four Fortify positions, and up to four builders that stay on what they built, pulled away only when their side is outnumbered at a base. A position whose upgrade was refused twice for the same reason is given up by the AI. Rate-limited log lines say what held a build up.
- A rich AI side digs in (#28): with the AI build budget on, a side with more than 600 RP banked raises the budget's share with the surplus, to twice its share at the 1000 RP cap (still at most 50%), and keeps the budget topped up from what it holds over 600 (still at most 100 RP), so its roadblocks, minefields and positions get built instead of the RP sitting at the cap. In overnight Everon games the leading side sat at the cap from about 105 minutes with nothing left to buy. At or below 600 RP nothing changes.
- A rich AI side fires more (#28): over 600 RP banked, by the same measure that raises its build budget (x1 at 600, x2 at the 1000 RP cap), it keeps RP for one more fire mission and counts air and rocket strikes whatever its doctrine, up to twice its doctrine's fire-support cap; it shells the attackers of a base that is merely in a fight (at the cap, from half its defence) rather than only one that is losing; it calls gun runs, rocket strikes and air preparation at lower threats and against fewer defenders (half at the cap); and its forward observers call missions more often. So the surplus turns into fire missions instead of sitting at the cap. At or below 600 RP nothing changes.
- A rich AI side buys quality, not quantity (#28): over 600 RP banked its purchases lean towards dearer units, more the richer it is (at the 1000 RP cap a 76 RP APC is three times as likely, a 12 RP team half as likely), armed vehicles are held back less, and when it needs a transport it takes an armed one such as an APC over a plain truck when it can. The squad cap is unchanged, and refills still come before any purchase.
- AI vehicle crews stay aboard on fast moves (#57): the crew of an armed vehicle no longer climb out at a gate or to heal a crewmate while their squad's order is in Fast mode, as they already didn't in Combat mode. Before, every Fast order counted as a player's order to Better Battle Buddies, which leaves those alone, so on AI commanders' attack moves and reinforcement runs LAV and BTR crews got out at gates and to treat a wounded commander. Orders a player gives a squad they command directly, and Game Master orders, still let them get out.
- A vehicle crewman Better Battle Buddies turned out of his hatch on a quiet stretch goes back in when a Fast or player order arrives, instead of riding into the fight turned out (Better Battle Buddies #7).
- AI squads can use squad tactics inside an objective (#31; host option, off by default): with `-acrSquadTactics 1`, once an attacking squad is inside the base it was sent to take, Better Battle Buddies' squad tactics run it (assault by bounds, flank a dug-in enemy, hold when outnumbered, search when contact is lost), never leaving the base's ring; the AI commander still picks the target, stages the squads and sets the base of fire, and squads staging or riding are left alone. Squads defending a base on a Defend order may bound to cover or push back an enemy inside the base, never chasing out of it (garrisons in buildings stay put). Off by default because AI-vs-AI test games showed no clear gain yet; Better Battle Buddies' squad tactics are being revisited first.
- AI building budget (host option, off by default): `-acrAIBuildShare N` makes each AI commander set N% (at most 30) of the RP it gains aside for engineering, up to 100 RP. Unit purchases leave that money alone, so its roadblocks, minefields (`-acrMinesAI`) and Fortify positions (`-acrFortifyAI`) get built instead of losing out to the next squad. The AI status log line shows the budget. The setup screen has a row for it: **AI build budget** (off, 10, 15 or 25% of income).
- AI minefields go where they do most: mixed fields across the road 60-150 m ahead of the side's own roadblock, where its gunners cover them, more on the enemy's road route and at chokepoints; AP fields in dead ground and tree lines in front of its positions. The spots are scored and tried best first, the old ring last, so the AI never lays fewer fields than before.
- The AI picks its FOB sites by score instead of taking the first that fits: every one of the old spots back from the front base that is clear is scored for room (the HQ and all its upgrade and defence spots clear), concealment from the enemy base and its approach, a road close by for the trucks, the distance back from the front, cover from our roadblocks and Fortify positions, the enemy's road route (off it is better) and towns (outside is better). Never fewer FOBs: the old first fit is always one of the candidates. The search is spread over a few strategy ticks.
- AI Fortify positions go where they see the approach: spots 80-200 m out towards the enemy are scored on their field of fire from a gunner's eye, slight high ground, no dead ground close in front, cover behind (counted twice), upgrades that fit, and overlooking the side's own roadblock or field, and tried best first. Spots on paved ground score less for every point of the position on it, spots on an airfield (by its runway lights, runway decals or an apron's concrete panels) and spots walled in by a building or other tall structure within 30 m in front (its field of fire stops there) score least, so the edge of open ground with cover behind wins; in a playtest both of a side's starting positions went on Arland's airfield, its longest field of fire, and a position faced a wall. The first spot tried is picked at random among those within 12% of the best score (seeded from the match's seed), so the AI doesn't dig in on the same spot every match. The old line spots are still tried, so the AI never digs fewer positions than before.
- AI choices vary between matches: where an AI choice is made from scored options, it is picked at random among those within 12% of the best (the best most likely, never a poor one), seeded from the match's seed so the same seed makes the same choices. `-acrVariety 0` always takes the best. The AI's Fortify spots use it first.
- AI attacks only count the squads that can get there: on foot within 2.1 km, in their own vehicles, by a taxi they can actually get (each taxi counted once), or by a ready air lift. A target beyond their reach is skipped, and the forming up waits for the slowest squad's walk or ride, up to 20 minutes. Before, squads 2-3 km off were sent on foot and the attack went in before they came (#27).
- An AI squad that left its roadblock or Fortify position to defend a base goes back to hold it once that defence is over, before a new attack can take it: in a playtest two holders went to defend, then onto an attack, and their positions stood empty for 15 minutes. It goes back only while the position is still ours, standing and unmanned, the doctrine's holders allow it, and within 20 minutes of leaving. The "waits for a free squad" log lines for AI builds now say what the side's free squads are (unarmed vehicles, vehicle crews, support teams, the reserve, due to refit, on other jobs, unfit for the job, in reach but not free for it, further out) and how many of the rest are defending, attacking or busy otherwise.

### Engineering

- Fortify positions get defenders: tier I holds 1 of the side's garrison soldiers in reserve at the position, tier II 2 and tier III 4 (an upgrade adds the difference). Like a base's garrison they deploy when an enemy comes within 400 m, defend the position, pack up once none is within 450 m and are used up as they die; a position that is gone takes its reserve with it. Sizes: `-acrFortifyDefenders1` / `-acrFortifyDefenders2` / `-acrFortifyDefenders3` (0 for none). Checkpoints can get them too with `-acrRoadblockDefenders N` (off by default).
- Sandbag positions, barbed wire and barricades lie on sloping ground instead of standing dead level: tilted to the ground under the ends of their footprint, at most 12 degrees. On a slope the site checks accept, one end used to float while the other was buried. Tents, buildings, bunkers, towers, checkpoints and gun nests stay upright. `-acrGroundTilt 0` places everything level as before.

### The battlefield

- Gunners stay on held guns (on by default; setup row **Gunners stay on held guns**, `-acrHoldGuns 0` for vanilla): a squad holding a spot (Defend, a checkpoint, a Fortify position, FOB defences) keeps its men on the static guns there. Vanilla took a gunner off his gun whenever anything he knew of, even a minute old, or just the sound of firing, was outside the gun's arc, so checkpoint and nest guns kept standing empty. Now he leaves only for an enemy he saw in the last 10 s within 60 m (a real flank), and a held gun that stands empty for 8 s is manned again by the nearest man of the squad.
- A Fortify position's guns stay manned while its squad is away: when the squad holding a tier II or III position leaves it for another job (laying mines in front, say), men from the position's reserve defenders man its guns, and they go back into the reserve once a squad holds it again. The squad goes as ordered. Before, the nest stood empty until the squad came back (playtest).

### Fixes

- Base garrisons no longer spawn inside walls (#73). FIA at the resistance pockets kept appearing stuck in houses and walls. Each garrison, patrol and guard soldier now gets a spot with room to stand, out in the open and on ground soldiers can walk; with none nearby, the nearest walkable spot to the base centre. Soldiers with a window post still go straight to it.
- The command map no longer floods the log with "DrawPolygon triangulation failed" (up to 2,000 lines a second when zoomed in on Everon): status bars and dots off the screen aren't drawn.
- A ride waits at the drop until its AI riders are out (up to 20 s, as it already did for a player). Before, the transport drove home as soon as it dropped them off, and an LAV, whose rear seats don't let men out once it moves, carried some of them home (#23). A man still aboard after the dismount's second try is taken out without a door; in the LAV's rear seats, where every exit was refused (the game said nothing was pending), his vehicle action queue is cleared first, and as a last resort he moves to another seat and gets out from there.
- A stuck vehicle giving a ride re-routes like a soft vehicle instead of holding where it is like a fighting one. A ride never takes a vehicle with a manned gun, so an LAV taxi that held only dropped the ride, with its riders still aboard (#23).
- Arland now starts on a dedicated server set up with a server config (the usual way to host). The Arland scenario and the Western medic shared one resource ID, so the server loaded the medic instead of the mission and stopped (#41). The medic has its own ID now; the scenario's ID is unchanged.
- AI FOB builds are no longer given up while the construction truck is still driving to a far site. The build's 3-minute give-up clock ran from when the truck was sent, so a site more than about 3 minutes' drive away was abandoned on the way. Now every 10 m the truck gets closer counts as progress, and a truck that stops getting closer (stuck or blocked) still gives up 3 minutes later. In Everon soaks, 8 builds were given up in 2 hours and USSR built no FOB; with the fix none was given up in 100 minutes, USSR had its FOB at 22 minutes and US had FOBs at 53 and 73 minutes.
- An AI side no longer marks the site of a FOB it has just built as abandoned. For up to 2 seconds after a build finished, before the FOB appeared, the AI could take the build for one that was given up, so it avoided the new FOB's own site for 10 minutes and wouldn't rebuild a lost HQ there in that time.
- A map marker removed while a player held the mouse on it (ACA rebuilds its markers as squads change) no longer throws an exception every frame: the drag just ends (pn11).
- The squads of an AI attack form up on separate approach lines again (#52). Moving a staging point out of the objective's sight could turn it up to 20 degrees towards a neighbouring line, so later squads' lines no longer fitted and the fourth squad of an attack was sent to the first squad's staging point. A point moved out of sight now keeps clear of every other line, taken or not.
- A vehicle waiting for its own crew to get in is no longer taken as stuck (#53). In a playtest an LAV's gunner got out at a gate; while he climbed back in, the LAV counted as stuck, backed up away from him twice, then held where it was and dropped the player's move order. Now the stuck clock waits while one of the crew is getting in or out or standing by the vehicle (up to a minute, so a real stall still shows), a new order starts the clock afresh, and back-ups only add up towards giving up until the vehicle gets 40 m past the spot where it was stuck. The log says when a vehicle is waiting for its crew. Under fire the old limits apply.
- AI squads in contact are left to BBB's fire and manoeuvre: ACA's own automatic flank of a stalled contact is switched off (#54). It hardly ever started (twice in 11 playtests), because a squad's own gunfire made it count as pinned down, and two mods steering the same squad in a firefight could only fight each other.

### Command and setup

- Company starting kits (#38): the setup screen has a **Blue company** and a **Red company** row (`-acrCompanyWest` / `-acrCompanyEast`), and every side gets its company's kit at Start, whoever plays it. The kit takes the place of part of its starting RP, so every company starts with the same value. Rifle Company (the default): the standard start. Mechanized: an APC or IFV instead of its truck. Fire Support (`weapons`): a mortar team and a forward observer team. Combat Engineers (`engineers`): two tier I positions at its front bases, on the best scored spots facing the enemy. Recon: two recon teams, and its intel on the enemy commander's doctrine starts at a hint. The kit is bought at list price, and an item its list lacks or it can't afford is left out.
- AC:A Think (hosts and admins): an **AC:A Think** button in the map's Debug box, or `/acr think [west|east|off] [log]` in chat anywhere, opens a card showing what each AI commander is thinking, live: squads free, defending and attacking, each attack's phase, staging points and base of fire, RP, support and build budget and doctrine; then either every squad of that side (task, order, target, how long since its last order and how many it got in 5 minutes, flagged when that looks like a loop, and whether it is recovering a dropped move) or the commander's last 20 decisions. The button cycles West squads, West decisions, East squads, East decisions and off. Only the player looking gets the updates, every 3 s while the card is open.
- The RTS camera has a Minefield button next to Roadblock: it cycles AP, AT and mixed mines (and off), the toast gives the price, and a right-click lays them with the selected squad, as the map panel's minefield orders do (#4).
- The RTS camera has a **Fortify** button next to Minefield: armed, a right-click digs the selected squad in there, or upgrades our position there, through the map's own Fortify order (its tier, facing and price); the toast gives the tier I price. Arming any other mode turns it off.
- The RTS camera aims too: with Fortify, Roadblock or a minefield armed, a right-drag aims the order instead of turning the camera. It goes where the button went down and faces where it came up, with a gold arrow on the ground while you drag; a plain right-click orders as before.
- The RTS camera shows where our squads are going and what our side has spotted, as the command map does (#66). Every squad's current order and its queued points are drawn from the squad in the order's colour, with a ring on each point of the selected squads; other squads' lines are fainter and left out beyond 1.5 km. Enemies our side has spotted get a red diamond at their last known spot, labelled with what they are, how long ago they were seen and how far away they are, and they fade once they're old news until our side forgets them (2.5 km at most). Right-click one with a squad selected to hunt it, as on the map.
- The second-in-command, its core (#14, `-acr2IC 1`, off by default): on a side a player commands, or one with no AI commander, every squad nobody has claimed is the AI's, and it runs them with the AI commander's own logic: attacks, defenders, grabs, posting idle squads, roadblocks and minefields. A player claims a squad by leading it or giving it an order, and hands it back with the squad panel's **AI commands this squad** toggle; the FOB panel's **AI manages this FOB** hands a FOB over for its supplies and upgrades. The squads it runs carry an [AI] tag on the map. It builds only out of the AI build budget, never from the side's RP, and a squad handed back gets its own move mode back (its attacks and rides run Fast). A side the AI commander leads never has one, its start-up hold included.
- Patrol (map panel, a new row of standing orders): a squad walks its queued points in a loop. Give it a move order, Shift + right-click more points, then click **Patrol**; the point it is heading for joins the loop, and Defend or Garrison points are walked through. With one point it walks between where it stands and that point; a squad standing still sets off at once. A new order or clicking Patrol again ends it. The squad's status shows "patrolling N points".
- Drag to aim: roadblocks, Fortify and the minefields still face the threat on a plain right-click. Right-click and drag sets the facing instead: the order goes where you pressed and faces where you let go, with a gold arrow on the map while you drag (while one of these orders is armed, a right-drag aims instead of panning the map; the keyboard and the screen edges still pan). A dragged minefield off the road lies square to the arrow, one on a road runs along it the way you dragged; an upgrade of a Fortify position keeps its facing (the order says so). The facing is sent with the order, so it works the same for every player in multiplayer; the AI commander keeps facing the threat.
- The AI doctrine follows the company (#38): the setup screen's Blue/Red AI doctrine rows default to **follows company**. Combat Engineers fight as Fortress, Mechanized as Blitz, Fire Support as Firepower, Recon as Raider, and a Rifle Company as Balanced (before: a random doctrine). Picking a doctrine on the row, random included, overrides it. That doctrine is the AI's home style: a doctrine it changed to (for losing ground or heavy losses) is kept while that trouble lasts, and once it is over the AI goes back home, never inside the 30-minute hold and after a varied 0-20 minutes more (seeded by the match; 10 with `-acrVariety 0`). A known enemy's counter still moves it on. The company and doctrine rows show only the name, and what it means is on a line under the tab's rows, so each fits its button at 1080p.

### Support

- A mortar team sets up out of the way of vehicles: not on or within 5 m of a road, and not within 30 m of a base's centre, where bought vehicles appear (#33). It looks up to 40 m round for such a spot (24 m before).
- A mortar team whose last two fire missions fired nothing takes no more missions until its tube is set up again (the support list says why). An AI commander's team moves a short way and sets up again by itself. Before, the AI kept paying for or queueing missions on a team that couldn't fire, every few minutes.
- A helicopter whose engine or rotor was shot up while it stood on its pad is repaired by servicing. Before, it looked fully healthy, wasn't repaired, and went back into servicing every minute for the rest of the game (the side couldn't use it or buy another). Vehicles serviced at depots and on reinforcement get the same full repair.

## 0.5.7 — released

Dig in and read the enemy. Fortify positions, mine the approaches, size your checkpoints, and face AI commanders that fight to their own doctrine and change it when it fails.

### Engineering

- Fortify position: a commander order that digs an infantry squad in where you right-click, facing the nearest known enemy or enemy base. Ordering it again on the position upgrades it from hasty (a line of three sandbag positions behind a barbed-tape fence, 10 RP) to dug in (an MG nest in place of the middle position, so the gun is in the centre of the line, and a sandbag bunker under a camo net, 15 RP) to a strongpoint (a bunker, and a heavy MG on a flank turned in across the front for crossfire, 25 RP). Pieces avoid big rocks, and each gun is placed where nothing but wire blocks its line of fire (a gun may aim a little up rising ground). An upgrade goes in whole or not at all: its pieces look further afield when their usual places are blocked (a road, rocks, a slope), and an upgrade that still can't fit is refused when you order it, with the reason and nothing charged. The AI commander picks sites where its upgrades will fit and leaves a position alone for a while after an upgrade there is refused. Work pauses while contested and runs twice as fast with engineers; the squad then holds the position and mans its guns, and the position shows on its side's map until its pieces are gone. `-acrFortify 0` turns the order off and `-acrFortifyAI 1` lets AI commanders fortify their approaches; both are on the startup Logistics settings page and in the mission header.
- Commander minefield orders: infantry squads lay AP, AT or mixed minefields (15 / 20 / 25 RP) and disarm and lift mines (3 RP) from the map panel. AT mines go in both lanes of the road through the field and AP mines off it, minefield signs mark the corners, and the area shows on the owning side's command map. Work pauses while contested and runs twice as fast with engineers or a construction truck; a cancelled order refunds the share not laid. The owning side's AI never sets off its own mines unless `-acrMinesRealistic 1`. `-acrMines 0` turns the orders off and `-acrMinesAI 1` lets AI commanders mine their approaches; all three are on the startup Logistics settings page and in the mission header.
- Minefield orders survive a regroup: a squad that stops to regroup on the way to its field now carries on and lays it afterwards. Before, the regroup cancelled the order and refunded it, and the squad then stood at the site doing nothing until it got a new order.
- Minefield orders no longer time out after a long pause: time with the order set aside (a regroup, a player holding the squad) doesn't count towards the walk limit or the 3-minute give-up timer, so the order isn't abandoned when it resumes.
- Roadblock sizes and barricades (a port of the ACA Field Engineering add-on): the map panel's Roadblock button becomes **Roadblock S / M / L** and **Barricade**. The small checkpoint has no machine gun (12 RP, 35 s), the medium is the old roadblock (20 RP, 45 s), the large carries a heavy machine gun (30 RP, 70 s), and barricades are sized to the road (8 RP, 25 s). RHS AFRF now builds the Soviet set, and FIA builds log barricades. A construction truck with the squad pays in supplies (75 / 225 / 350 / 40) when it has them, else RP. A build called off is refunded where it was paid from, Halt included (it used not to refund). The 3-minute give-up timer starts when the squad reaches the road (the walk has its own limit), and the roadblock waits for vehicles to leave the spot. AI commanders still build medium checkpoints paid in RP.
- Roadblocks ordered by a player face the threat: the nearest enemy their side knows of within 600 m, else the nearest enemy base it can see (the AI commander's already faced the enemy base). Before, the checkpoint faced whichever way the road happened to run.
- Roadblocks snap to the road centre instead of staying at the clicked position; clearance checks use the actual construction site.
- Minefield, roadblock and Fortify orders given to a squad still riding in a vehicle now carry on once the squad is out. Before, getting out cancelled the order and refunded it.

### AI commanders

- AI commander doctrines: each AI side fights to one of five styles for the whole match. Balanced is the old commander; Blitz goes mechanised and attacks early; Firepower leans on mortars, gunships and preparation fire; Fortress defends in depth and counterattacks lost bases at once; Raider sends small teams after weak bases and FOBs. New setup rows "Blue AI doctrine" and "Red AI doctrine" (random by default). No doctrine fields a bigger army.
- Doctrines now shape the whole commander: rides and air lifts, defence in depth and how long defenders hold, defensive air strikes, mortar fire missions and forward observers, base-of-fire time, minefields, FOB placement, refits and FIA pockets. Firepower really keeps RP for two fire missions now, and Raider no longer re-attacks the base that just beat it or fields fewer men than the others.
- Enemy doctrines can be discovered: contacts, enemy FOBs found and bases changing hands build up intel until the side gets a hint ("Blitz or Raider?") and then a confirmed doctrine, shown in the map panel's Report. Players under an AI commander see their own commander's doctrine there.
- AI commanders change doctrine when theirs isn't working (losing ground, heavy losses, stalemate), or to counter an enemy doctrine they have confirmed. The other side only notices once fresh intel shows the change. New setup row "AI doctrines adapt" / `-acrDoctrineAdapt 0` to keep the first doctrine.
- AI commanders keep spending in long games: the squad cap counts only fighting units (trucks, ambulances and 1-man remnants no longer fill it), and badly hurt idle squads walk back from the front to a quiet base to refill instead of staying at their post at a third of their strength.
- AI squads become available for combat orders after finishing a roadblock, instead of being tied to it indefinitely. Roadblock construction leaves the commander's reserve squad available.
- Recon teams (snipers, scouts, spotters) take overwatch on the front, armoured vehicles hold a standoff ring around an objective instead of driving into it, and mortars move 15 seconds after a fire mission. Heavily defended targets get gunship and rocket preparation fire.

### The battlefield

- FIA pockets of resistance: on whole-map and half-map battles a few empty bases away from both HQs start held by an FIA garrison (bigger at military sites, which also keep a small patrol). They look unclaimed until your units get close, so a squad sent to grab an empty village may have a fight on its hands. Clear one to capture it. New setup row "FIA resistance": few (default), many or off.
- Generated battles vary tactical objectives and select later income sites together while preserving opening income, earning-site count, total RP and spacing. Consecutive seeds cycle evenly through small complete sets of valid income arrangements. Flashpoints vary their local area and objective mix, keeping 5-7 accessible sites and protected opening objectives.
- Generated half-map battles use varied diagonal, shifted and compact regions, with more suitable HQ arrangements across seeds. Existing objective access, spacing and opening-income safeguards remain in place.
- RU, UA and RHS USMC requisitions use distinct infantry formations with revised prices, dedicated support ammunition and role-specific replacement kits. UA keeps mostly Soviet equipment alongside Western-equipped squads and fire teams. Rare Special Forces carry suppressed weapons, modern armor and night vision without thermal sights; AI selection does not increase with their price. Marines use combat optics, dedicated scout/engineer/sniper teams and rare suppressed MARSOC; shared utility and logistics vehicles use aligned prices.
- Fixed UA and Russian Typhoon purchases failing to deploy because their driver and gunner crews never spawned.

### Command and setup

- The setup screen is sorted into tabs: **Battlefield** (sides, battlefield, seed, the map preview, FIA resistance and threat), **Command**, **Economy**, **Logistics** and **Engineers**. The map preview now sits right under the seed it shows, and a line at the top sums up the match (sides, battlefield, seed) from every tab. Before, the list could run off a 1080p screen, and the map preview was a page away from the seed. A screen too short for a tab still gets a page button. The tabs and rows use the command panel's blue buttons, so the row under the mouse no longer turns white on white.
- Shift + right-click on a squad that is holding where it arrived now sends it there at once. Before, the point was queued behind its hold, which never ends, so it never went (the panel said "waypoint added" and the squad stood still).
- Fixed the 3D commander camera missing its mouse cursor and movement controls. WASD pans, right-drag rotates and the wheel changes altitude; exiting restores the previous view.
- 3D commander camera: each mouse-wheel notch is a small altitude step, panning speeds up with height, and friendly squads and bases get on-screen markers with name and distance that you can click to select.
- Taking over a soldier in a squad on a ride no longer ends the ride and sends the transport home. The truck waits at the pickup and drop-off for a player rider, and F8 puts the riders back on the ride's order. After death you return to the squad you possessed, not the last squad you ordered.
- Host and Play launchers start every Better Battle Buddies behaviour switched on, and its options sit in one BBB submenu instead of separate main-menu toggles.

### Air lifts

- Off-map lift helicopters arrive already airborne with rotors spun up, saving about 20 seconds. When a squad needs two helicopters and only one landing spot fits, the second holds short until the first has unloaded and left.
- Air-lift passengers receive individual boarding assignments for both helicopters, so soldiers who cannot fit in the first Huey walk to the second. Scripted placement is reserved for stalled boarding.
- Two air lifts no longer pick landing spots within 40 m of each other, and loose weapons and gear dropped at a landing zone no longer stop a helicopter touching down.

### Fixes

- Fixed late-game match stalls and infinite supply truck pathfinding loops: AI commanders scale active attack fronts (up to 8) and unit caps on large maps when holding surplus territory and RP, dismounted infantry no longer embark on multi-kilometer cross-island grabs on foot, and supply trucks en route to bases avoid redundant 15-second route recalculations.

## 0.5.6 — released

Take the fight forward. Build a base behind your advance, keep it supplied and defend it against an AI commander that can build, reinforce and raid too.

### Build, supply and defend

- Establish FOB turns a squad with a construction truck and 500 supplies into a forward-base builder. Pick a clear site on the map and keep the squad there for the 90-second build. Cancelling refunds the reserved supplies; losing the truck loses them. Stalled construction gives up with a refund after three minutes without progress.
- Develop each FOB through eight upgrade spots: hospital, maintenance, ammo, fuel, helipad, radio relay, barracks and supply depot. Maintenance and barracks have two tiers. Upgrades use the FOB's supplies, build faster with engineers nearby and pause while contested. Parked vehicles must clear a finished building's spot before it appears.
- Fortify the perimeter through three Defences tiers: sandbags, machine-gun nests, then bunkers and guard towers. Blocked positions are skipped. Barracks send part of the garrison on perimeter patrols, and eligible AI defenders can man the guns when BBB static weapons are enabled.
- Blocked upgrade spots are identified individually, and matching buildings already inside the FOB count toward its upgrades. FIA keeps its smaller maintenance and barracks models at the higher tier, with the full upgrade effect.
- The AI commander establishes FOBs near a distant front, develops and resupplies them while keeping an RP reserve, deploys from suitable forward bases and rebuilds lost headquarters.

### Raid, capture or rebuild

- AI sappers can plant a real demolition charge at an undefended enemy FOB headquarters, withdraw and detonate it after 45 seconds. Defenders can use the normal Disarm action; AI defenders are sent to the charge too.
- Players can destroy an enemy headquarters with an M112 or TSh-400 detonated within 10 m. Shovel dismantling requires the FOB to be contested with no defenders within 30 m.
- A destroyed headquarters cooks off with a heavy blast and secondary explosions. Stay clear: the main blast is lethal nearby and can wound out to about 30 m. AI sappers wait for their squad to get clear before firing.
- Losing headquarters takes the FOB offline: deployment, reinforcement, services, deliveries and upgrades stop. Surviving buildings remain and transfer with a captured FOB. Establish FOB inside a friendly offline base rebuilds its headquarters.

### A more coordinated opponent

- Attacks keep a base of fire in overwatch even before enemies are spotted at the objective. It fires at seen enemies and suppresses reported contacts instead of shooting at an empty objective centre.
- Assault squads form up on separate approach lines and commit together, giving nearby reinforcements time to arrive. Distant latecomers no longer trickle into an assault already under way; specialist support teams stay out of the assault force.
- Threatened AI bases defend in depth: some defenders move 100-300 m toward a spotted attack while others stay in reserve. Forward squads fall back when overrun, badly hurt or no longer needed.
- Improved AI purchasing and reinforcement decisions, including saving for complete units and avoiding repeated construction-truck purchases.

### Transport and fixes

- Air lifts search farther for a second landing spot when a squad needs two helicopters. Helicopters bank through turns and change pitch more smoothly; landing checks no longer treat nearby soldiers as rotor-blocking obstacles.
- Improved gunner-drive hill starts and recovery from stalled or unreachable vehicle orders.
- Gunners stay on a stopped vehicle's working, loaded gun when its driver is lost under fire; passenger squads can still dismount. Fixed soldiers repeatedly mounting and abandoning parked guns.
- Fixed FOB upgrades being invisible to joined players, destroyed antennas blocking relay replacement, overlapping construction wasting supplies and headquarters spawning over the construction truck. Blocked completions refund reserved supplies. FOB construction and removal now update nearby AI navigation.
- Planted demolition fuses survive command handovers and cancelled attack plans.
- Squads use an awake soldier as their reference when the leader is unconscious. Unconscious soldiers no longer hold, contest or spot bases, or block FOB construction. Fixed reserve squads repeatedly abandoning their regroup order.
- LAV commander positions count as crew and stay aboard during passenger dismounts. RHS rifles with folded backup sights now raise them when no optic is fitted.
